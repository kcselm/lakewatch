# Herons Implementation Plan

> **For agentic workers:** REQUIRED SUB-SKILL: Use superpowers:subagent-driven-development (recommended) or superpowers:executing-plans to implement this plan task-by-task. Steps use checkbox (`- [ ]`) syntax for tracking.

**Goal:** Make the Great Blue Heron and the Great Egret detailed birds in their late-September plumages, with necks that bend like a heron's, standing in a new wading shelf on the far shore and feeding in a heron's style.

**Architecture:**
- **The builder:** an optional chain of neck bones along the traced neck, posed by rotation between named shapes (hunch, the trace, erect, strike), so the neck keeps its length; `PoseState.headPitch`; a reach that can aim above the ground (a heron on the bed striking into the water); and a version-only part that hangs from the head (the adult Great Blue Heron's plumes). Every existing bird builds and poses exactly as before.
- **The behaviour:** a `heron` style on `wade`: wait with the neck coiled, stalk, strike with a ring, swallow after a catch, stretch up alert, hunch between bouts, and keep a heron's distance from the other herons.
- **The scene:** a shelf of shallow lake bed along the far waterline and a `far-shallows` zone on it, where the herons stand.
- **The birds:** the Great Blue Heron traced first, the worked example; then the Great Egret by its method. Each is held to a test file written here from its research.

**Tech Stack:** Vite 8, TypeScript 7, Vitest 5 (`environment: 'node'`), Three.js 0.186, zod 4, Preact.

**Spec:** `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-27-herons-design.md`. Read it first.
- Every number and mark comes from the two research documents, `docs/research/2026-09-27-great-blue-heron.md` and `docs/research/2026-09-27-great-egret.md`: section 9 holds the numbers for the model, 10 the test thresholds, 11 the style's settings, and 8 the tracing references.
- The detailed builder is described in `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-24-detailed-birds-design.md`, and its swimming additions in `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-26-swimmers-design.md`.
- The previous round's plan, `docs/superpowers/plans/2026-09-26-swimmers.md`, shows the conventions this one follows; its Task 14 is the fullest account of tracing a bird in the viewer.

## Decisions made while planning

- **The spare neck joints sit at `neckMid`, not at the head joint** (Task 1). The chain's extra bones hang between `neckMid` and `head`; parked at `neckMid` with no length, they leave the head's offset from its parent what it was, so every shipped bird's fingerprint is unchanged and the two-joint neck's scaling means what it meant.
- **A negative `reachDepth` aims above the ground** (Task 1). A heron's feet stand on the bed and its reach aims relative to them, so its strike into the water above the bed needs one. No shipped behaviour sets a negative value; the one swim test that pinned the old clamp keeps its other checks.
- **A part's kind names its bone** (Task 2): a `curl` hangs from the body and a `plume` from the head. That meets the spec's "names the bone it hangs from" with nothing more in the schema.
- **A plume's droop is measured from the horizontal as traced** (Task 2). Measured from the head's own line, the strands lay inside the back of the upper neck.
- **The style comes before the shelf** (Tasks 4 and 5; the spec had them the other way round), because the shelf's zone test reads the style's water band.
- **The neck-shape picker has a fifth shape, the swallow** (Task 3): erect with the head pitched up, the pose the style uses after a catch.
- **`far-shore` is removed** (Task 5): nothing but the herons used it.
- **The water band is 5–22 cm, not 5–30** (Tasks 4 and 5), and the spec's leg rule changes with it. Both herons most often feed 15–25 cm deep (Willard 1977); the spec's "no deeper than the shorter-legged heron's tarsus" would have kept them at 14 cm. The rule is now "leave 25 mm of the egret's bare tibia above the water", which gives 22 cm; Tasks 6 and 7 test each species' legs against it.
- **Herons keep their own species' distance and a shorter one from other herons** (Task 4): Great Blue Herons 10 m apart, egrets 5 m, a heron and an egret 3 m. The research found each defending room against its own kind, and the two species feeding within 1–3 m of each other on the valley's photos. The spec's "two herons are held to the larger of their two spacings" holds within each kind of pair; the spawner gives each species' herons a group, as it does a flock, to tell the pairs apart.
- **The hunch and the alert tilt the body up** (Task 4, `rest.lean`, `alert.lean`): the photographed resting and erect herons hold the body steeply up, not level.
- **The style's timings are tuned to the sourced strike rates** (Task 4), and a test holds each species' long-run rate in its range: the Great Blue Heron 0.2–0.3 a minute, the egret 0.4–0.9.
- **The cast is two of each** (Task 8): an adult and a juvenile Great Blue Heron (60:40 in the valley's September records) and two Great Egrets (one version: the ages look alike in September).

How a chained neck poses:
- `neckExtend` 0 is the `hunch`, 0.5 the traced posture (every bend 0), 1 `erect`. Each joint's bend blends
  linearly between neighbouring shapes, in degrees, and the joints only rotate, so every segment keeps its length and
  the painted marks stay where they were painted.
- A `reach` of `w` blends each bend on toward `strike` by `smooth(w)`. Then `reachChain` turns the base joint just far
  enough for the bill tip, with the head tipped as far as `reachAngle` lets it, to meet its mark (`probeDepth` plus
  `reachDepth` below the ground), and tips the head onto it, as `reachDown` does for a neck of two joints.
- `headPitch` turns the head last, on every bird. At 0 it changes nothing.

Why the spare joints sit where they do (a decision made while planning; the spec said "at the head joint"): the chain's
extra bones hang between `neckMid` and `head`. A bird without a chain parks them at `neckMid`, with no length and no
rotation, so the head's offset from its parent is exactly what its offset from `neckMid` was, and `poseNeck`'s scaling
of the head's position means what it always meant. Multiplying by an identity matrix is exact in floating point, so
every shipped bird's fingerprint stays the same, as Step 3 checks. A chained bird parks the joints it doesn't use at
its last joint before the head.

```ts
import * as THREE from 'three'
import type { DetailedBirdModel } from './build'
import { RINGS, RADIAL } from './loft'

/**
 * The loft's rings as the model's last pose() left them, group space: RINGS rows of RADIAL points, tail to bill base.
 * The loft is the first thing the builder meshes, so its rings are the geometry's first RINGS × RADIAL vertices.
 */
export function posedRings(m: DetailedBirdModel): THREE.Vector3[][] {
  if (m.anatomy.closed[0]?.name !== 'loft' || m.anatomy.closed[0].indexFirst !== 0) throw new Error('the loft is meshed first')
  m.group.updateMatrixWorld(true)
  return Array.from({ length: RINGS }, (_, i) =>
    Array.from({ length: RADIAL }, (_, j) => m.mesh.getVertexPosition(i * RADIAL + j, new THREE.Vector3())))
}

/** A ring's centre, and the normal of its best-fit plane (Newell's), turned to face along the spine toward the bill. */
function ringPlane(ring: THREE.Vector3[], toward: THREE.Vector3): { c: THREE.Vector3; n: THREE.Vector3 } {
  const c = new THREE.Vector3()
  for (const p of ring) c.add(p)
  c.divideScalar(ring.length)
  const n = new THREE.Vector3()
  ring.forEach((a, j) => {
    const b = ring[(j + 1) % ring.length]!
    n.x += (a.y - b.y) * (a.z + b.z); n.y += (a.z - b.z) * (a.x + b.x); n.z += (a.x - b.x) * (a.y + b.y)
  })
  n.normalize()
  if (n.dot(toward) < 0) n.negate()
  return { c, n }
}

/**
 * The furthest any posed ring from `fromU` on reaches back behind the plane of the ring before it, metres (0 when none
 * does): worstFold, on the skin as posed. A neck bent tighter than its girth allows creases here first.
 */
export function posedFold(m: DetailedBirdModel, fromU = 0): number {
  const rings = posedRings(m)
  let worst = 0
  for (let i = Math.max(1, Math.floor(fromU * (RINGS - 1))); i < RINGS - 1; i++) {
    const ahead = centre(rings[i + 1]!).sub(centre(rings[i - 1]!))
    const { n } = ringPlane(rings[i]!, ahead)
    for (let j = 0; j < RADIAL; j++) worst = Math.max(worst, -rings[i + 1]![j]!.clone().sub(rings[i]![j]!).dot(n))
  }
  return worst
}

function centre(ring: THREE.Vector3[]): THREE.Vector3 {
  const c = new THREE.Vector3()
  for (const p of ring) c.add(p)
  return c.divideScalar(ring.length)
}

/** where the ray from p along d crosses triangle (a, b, c), as a multiple of d; NaN if it misses or runs behind p */
function rayHit(p: THREE.Vector3, d: THREE.Vector3, a: THREE.Vector3, b: THREE.Vector3, c: THREE.Vector3): number {
  const e1 = b.clone().sub(a), e2 = c.clone().sub(a)
  const h = d.clone().cross(e2)
  const det = e1.dot(h)
  if (Math.abs(det) < 1e-18) return NaN
  const s = p.clone().sub(a)
  const u = s.dot(h) / det
  if (u < 0 || u > 1) return NaN
  const q = s.cross(e1)
  const v = d.dot(q) / det
  if (v < 0 || u + v > 1) return NaN
  const t = e2.dot(q) / det
  return t > 0 ? t : NaN
}

/** a direction no face of a bird's loft lies along */
const RAY = new THREE.Vector3(0.5773, 0.5812, 0.5735).normalize()

/**
 * How deep the posed loft's skin passes through itself, metres (0 when it doesn't), over the rings from `fromU` on:
 * each skin vertex is pushed `eps` out along its normal, and if that point lies inside the closed loft (an odd number
 * of crossings along a ray), the skin there has passed into another part of the body, as deep as the point lies from
 * the nearest face of the loft more than `skip` rings away. `ring` is the vertex's ring, `into` the ring it went into.
 * A neck folded into a hunch, or reaching down past the breast, shows here.
 */
export function passThrough(m: DetailedBirdModel, fromU = 0, eps = 0.0005, skip = 2): { depth: number; ring: number; into: number } {
  const loft = m.anatomy.closed.find(c => c.name === 'loft')!
  const index = m.mesh.geometry.getIndex()!.array
```

```ts
/**
 * An adult heron's occipital plumes: two thin strands side by side, from just inside the top of the head at the
 * station `at`, trailing straight back `length`, `droop` degrees below the horizontal in the traced side view, and
 * sagging PLUME_SAG further by their tips. Closed tubes on the plume bone, which hangs from the head, so posed they
 * turn with it.
 */
export function buildPlume(m: Mesher, part: Extract<PartSpec, { kind: 'plume' }>, profile: Profile): PartResult {
  const indexFirst = m.indexCount
  const s = profile.sampleAt(profile.uOf(part.at) + (part.dx ?? 0) / profile.length)
  const up = new THREE.Vector3(s.axis.x, s.axis.y, 0), back = new THREE.Vector3(-1, 0, 0)
  const base = new THREE.Vector3(s.point.x, s.point.y, 0).addScaledVector(up, s.up - PLUME_RADIUS)
  const z = new THREE.Vector3(0, 0, 1)
  let top = base.clone()
  const STEPS = 16
  for (const side of [1, -1]) {
    const points: THREE.Vector3[] = [], radius: number[] = []
    const p = base.clone()
    for (let k = 0; k <= STEPS; k++) {
      if (k > 0) {
        // a positive turn about +z swings a line pointing back down: by the droop, and the sag so far
        const a = THREE.MathUtils.degToRad(part.droop + (PLUME_SAG * (k - 0.5)) / STEPS)
        p.addScaledVector(back.clone().applyAxisAngle(z, a), part.length / STEPS)
      }
      points.push(p.clone().setZ((side * PLUME_GAP) / 2))
      radius.push(PLUME_RADIUS + (PLUME_TIP_RADIUS - PLUME_RADIUS) * (k / STEPS))
      if (p.y > top.y) top = p.clone().setZ(0)
    }
    sweep(m, points, radius, 'plume', [['plume', 1]])
  }
  return { kind: part.kind, base, top, indexFirst, indexCount: m.indexCount - indexFirst }
}
```

What the style does, state by state:
- **wait:** the neck in the traced S (`neckExtend` 0.5). Now and then it peers down (`headPitch` below 0) or turns slowly
  on the spot.
- **stalk:** a few slow steps toward a spot `stalk.metres` off, in its zone and clear of the other herons, at
  `stalk.speed`; it walks only once it faces the spot, and stops short of a heron it would come too close to.
- **strike:** the neck drawn back to `drawNeck`, then `reach` eased to 1 along a smoothstep over `strike.down` seconds,
  the body tipped `strike.lean`, the bill aimed `strike.depth` under the surface (`reachDepth = min(0, ground +
  depth)`: the feet are on the bed at `ground(x, z)`, below 0, and the bill never goes into the mud); held, then
  recovered. One ring of size `strike.ring` starts `strike.ahead` in front of the feet as the bill goes in.
- **swallow:** only after a strike that caught (`swallow.chance`): the neck erect and the bill tipped up
  `swallow.pitch`.
- **alert:** the neck erect, the bill level, the body tilted up a little (`alert.lean`), still.
- **hunch:** after a feeding bout of `bout.seconds`, a rest of `rest.seconds` with the neck folded and the body tilted
  up (`rest.lean`): the photographed resting herons hold the body nearly upright (research section 6).

Every pose value eases toward what its state asks at a fixed rate a second, so nothing snaps; the strike's descent is
the smoothstep, which takes `down` seconds (six frames at 60 fps for the shortest, 0.1 s).

Spacing works as the swimmers' does, for herons only: the spawner hands every heron the others as `neighbours`, gives
each species' herons a shared `group` (the cast entry's, as it does a flock), and places each clear of those already
placed (`heronStart`, with the cast's own random source, as `startSpot` places the swimmers). A heron keeps its own
species' `spacing.same` from one of its group, and the larger of the two birds' `spacing.other` from any other heron:
Great Blue Herons 10 m apart, egrets 5 m, a heron and an egret 3 m (the research found the two species feeding within
1-3 m of each other on the valley's photos, and each defending room against its own kind).

The settings are the research's section 11. The shares and times are tuned so each species' long-run strike rate
lands in its sourced range (Great Blue Heron 0.2-0.3 a minute, Great Egret 0.4-0.9), and a test holds that.
`strike.ahead`, where the ring starts, is 0.3 m for now: Tasks 6 and 7 set it to where each built bird's bill goes in,
and their tests hold it there.

`validateCast` now ties every style to its behaviour: a probing style can only probe, a swimming style only swim, and a
wading style only wade, with the same message for each (the two old messages are unchanged word for word). A wader with
no style, the yellowlegs, wades as `wade` always has.

At the end of this task both block herons feed in the heron style. They still stand on the far shore's dry slope, where
`min(0, ground + depth)` puts the bill on the ground, until Task 5 moves them onto the shelf.

```ts
import type { BehaviorContext, BehaviorController, FlockGroup, Neighbour } from './types'
import { approach, between, ease, polygonOf, turnToward, wrap } from './types'
import { pointInPolygon, randomPointInPolygon } from '../location/zones'
import type { PoseState } from '../birds/model'
import type { PolygonZone } from '../location/schema'
import type { Rng } from '../catalog/types'

type Range = readonly [number, number]

/** A heron's settings: how it waits, stalks, strikes, and rests, and the room it keeps. */
export interface HeronSettings {
  /** standing still with the neck in the traced S, seconds; now and then it peers down, or turns slowly on the spot */
  wait: { seconds: Range; peer: { chance: number; seconds: Range; pitch: number }; turn: { chance: number; rate: number } }
  /** what ends a wait: shares of a strike, a stalk, and an alert (the rest, another wait) */
  next: { strike: number; stalk: number; alert: number }
  /** a stalk: a few slow steps toward a spot `metres` away, at `speed` m/s, the neck held at `neck` */
  stalk: { metres: Range; speed: number; neck: number }
  /**
   * a strike: the neck drawn back to `drawNeck` over `draw` seconds, then the reach eased all the way down over `down`
   * seconds, the bill `depth` metres under the water and no steeper than `reachAngle` below level, the body tipped
   * `lean`; held `hold` seconds, then it recovers. The bill goes in `ahead` metres in front of the feet, where the ring
   * of size `ring` starts.
   */
  strike: { draw: number; drawNeck: number; down: number; hold: number; depth: number; reachAngle: number; lean: number; ahead: number; ring: number }
  /** how often a strike catches, and the swallow after one: the neck erect and the bill tipped up `pitch` radians */
  swallow: { chance: number; seconds: Range; pitch: number }
  /** the neck erect, the bill level, the body tilted up by `lean` (negative: nose up), still */
  alert: { seconds: Range; lean: number }
  /** a feeding bout's length, then a rest hunched with the neck folded and the body tilted up by `lean`, seconds */
  bout: { seconds: Range }
  rest: { seconds: Range; lean: number }
  /** metres it keeps from a heron of its own species (`same`) and from any other heron (`other`) */
  spacing: { same: number; other: number }
}

type State = 'wait' | 'stalk' | 'strike' | 'swallow' | 'alert' | 'hunch'

/** how fast the neck, the head, and the lean ease toward what a state asks, per second */
const NECK_RATE = 2.5
const PITCH_RATE = 3
const LEAN_RATE = 3
/** how fast gait eases in and out, per second */
const GAIT_RATE = 8
/** the fastest it turns, rad/s */
const TURN = 0.8
/** a stalk ends this close to its spot, metres */
const ARRIVE = 0.1
/** a spot is clear of a neighbour by its spacing and this much more, metres */
const MARGIN = 0.2

/**
 * The Great Blue Heron, from docs/research/2026-09-27-great-blue-heron.md, section 11 (behaviour in section 7, the
 * poses in section 6). Sourced: the strike rate the shares and times give (0.2-0.3 a minute: Willard 1977, Bovino &
 * Burtt 1979), the catch rate (41%: Bovino & Burtt 1979), and the strike's bill angle (photo, 70-90 degrees). The
 * waits, the stalk's speed, the draw-back and strike times, the bouts and rests, the body's tilt at rest, and the
 * spacing are estimates the research marks as such. `strike.ahead` is where the built model's bill goes in (Task 6).
 */

  wait: { seconds: [10, 60], peer: { chance: 0.33, seconds: [1, 3], pitch: 0.25 }, turn: { chance: 0.3, rate: 0.15 } },
  next: { strike: 0.3, stalk: 0.5, alert: 0.1 },

  strike: { draw: 0.4, drawNeck: 0.35, down: 0.12, hold: 0.3, depth: 0.08, reachAngle: 1.4, lean: 0.45, ahead: 0.3, ring: 0.6 },
  swallow: { chance: 0.4, seconds: [1.5, 4], pitch: 0.6 },
  alert: { seconds: [4, 15], lean: -0.2 },
  bout: { seconds: [600, 1500] },
  rest: { seconds: [180, 600], lean: -0.35 },
  spacing: { same: 10, other: 3 },
}

 * Willard 1977), the catch rate (0.52-0.68: Moreno et al. 2005, Mincey 2006), and the strike's and swallow's bill

export const GREAT_EGRET: HeronSettings = {

  next: { strike: 0.4, stalk: 0.5, alert: 0.05 },
  stalk: { metres: [0.3, 1.5], speed: 0.05, neck: 0.6 },
  strike: { draw: 0.3, drawNeck: 0.35, down: 0.1, hold: 0.25, depth: 0.06, reachAngle: 1.2, lean: 0.45, ahead: 0.3, ring: 0.5 },
  swallow: { chance: 0.5, seconds: [1, 3], pitch: 0.4 },

const HERONS: Record<string, HeronSettings> = { grbher3: GREAT_BLUE_HERON, greegr: GREAT_EGRET }

/** The heron settings for a species; the Great Blue Heron's for any other. */
export function heronFor(species?: string): HeronSettings {
  return (species && HERONS[species]) || GREAT_BLUE_HERON
}

/**
 * The water the heron style stands in, metres deep: every point of a heron's zone lies in it (the zone-terrain test
 * holds that). Both herons most often feed 15-25 cm deep (Willard 1977); 22 cm is the deepest that leaves 25 mm of the
 * shorter-legged egret's bare tibia above the water (docs/research/2026-09-27-great-egret.md, section 6).
 */
export const HERON_WATER: Range = [0.05, 0.22]

/**
 * How close a heron lets another come: its own species' spacing for one of its own group (the spawner gives each
 * species' herons a group, as it does a flock), else the larger of the two birds' spacing for other herons.
 */
function keepFrom(n: Neighbour, group: FlockGroup | undefined, spacing: HeronSettings['spacing']): number {
  return group !== undefined && n.group === group ? spacing.same : Math.max(spacing.other, n.spacing ?? 0)
}

/**
 * The `heron` style on `wade`: it stands in the shallows with its neck coiled and waits, now and then stalking a few
 * slow steps, striking with a ring where the bill goes in, swallowing with its head tipped up after a catch, or
 * stretching up alert. Feeding bouts alternate with rests, hunched with the neck folded. It keeps its distance from the
 * other herons (ctx.neighbours), starting clear of them, choosing spots clear of them, and stopping short of them.
 */
export function createHeron(ctx: BehaviorContext, startX: number, startZ: number, S: HeronSettings = heronFor(ctx.species)): BehaviorController {
  const zone = polygonOf(ctx)
  let x = startX, z = startZ, heading = ctx.rng() * Math.PI * 2
  let state: State = 'wait'
  let timer = between(ctx.rng, S.wait.seconds)
  let bout = between(ctx.rng, S.bout.seconds)
  let tx = x, tz = z
  let strikeT = 0, rang = false, caught = false
  /** the neck and lean a strike's descent starts from */
  let from: { neck: number; lean: number } | null = null
  let peer = 0, turning = 0
  let reachDepth = 0
  const controller: BehaviorController & { spacing: number } = {
    name: 'wade',
    get state() { return state },
    get x() { return x }, get y() { return ctx.ground(x, z) }, get z() { return z }, get heading() { return heading },
    // what it keeps from herons of other species; its own species' spacing it reads from its settings
    spacing: S.spacing.other,
    group: ctx.group,
    update,
  }

  const others = (): Neighbour[] => (ctx.neighbours?.() ?? []).filter(n => n !== controller)
  const clearOf = (px: number, pz: number, extra: number) =>
    others().every(n => Math.hypot(n.x - px, n.z - pz) >= keepFrom(n, ctx.group, S.spacing) + extra)

  function begin(next: State): void {
    state = next
    switch (next) {
      case 'wait':
        timer = between(ctx.rng, S.wait.seconds)
        peer = ctx.rng() < S.wait.peer.chance ? between(ctx.rng, S.wait.peer.seconds) : 0
        turning = ctx.rng() < S.wait.turn.chance ? (ctx.rng() < 0.5 ? -1 : 1) : 0
        break
      case 'stalk': {
        // a spot a few metres off, in the zone and clear of the other herons, or wait again if none of a few tries is
        for (let i = 0; i < 12; i++) {
          const a = ctx.rng() * Math.PI * 2, d = between(ctx.rng, S.stalk.metres)
          const [px, pz] = [x + Math.cos(a) * d, z - Math.sin(a) * d]
          if (pointInPolygon(px, pz, zone.points) && clearOf(px, pz, MARGIN)) { tx = px; tz = pz; timer = 120; return }
        }
        begin('wait')
        break
      }
      case 'strike': {
        strikeT = 0; rang = false; from = null
        caught = ctx.rng() < S.swallow.chance
        // the bill aims `depth` under the surface (y = 0), from feet on the bed at ground(x, z) < 0, and never into the mud
        reachDepth = Math.min(0, ctx.ground(x, z) + S.strike.depth)
        timer = S.strike.draw + S.strike.down + S.strike.hold + 1
        break
      }
      case 'swallow': timer = between(ctx.rng, S.swallow.seconds); break
      case 'alert': timer = between(ctx.rng, S.alert.seconds); break
      case 'hunch': timer = between(ctx.rng, S.rest.seconds); break
    }
  }

  function next(): void {
    if (state === 'hunch') { bout = between(ctx.rng, S.bout.seconds); begin(ctx.rng() < 0.3 ? 'alert' : 'wait'); return }
    if (state === 'strike' && caught) { begin('swallow'); return }
    if (state !== 'wait') { begin('wait'); return }
    if (bout <= 0) { begin('hunch'); return }
    const r = ctx.rng()
    if (r < S.next.strike) begin('strike')
    else if (r < S.next.strike + S.next.stalk) begin('stalk')
    else if (r < S.next.strike + S.next.stalk + S.next.alert) begin('alert')
    else begin('wait')
  }

  function update(dt: number, pose: PoseState): void {
    timer -= dt
    if (state !== 'hunch') bout -= dt
    if (timer <= 0) next()
    let neck = 0.5, pitch = 0, lean = 0, gait = 0
    switch (state) {
      case 'wait': {
        if (peer > 0) { peer -= dt; pitch = -S.wait.peer.pitch }
        if (turning) heading = wrap(heading + turning * S.wait.turn.rate * dt)
        break
      }
      case 'stalk': {
        neck = S.stalk.neck
        gait = 1
        const want = Math.atan2(-(tz - z), tx - x)
        heading = turnToward(heading, want, TURN * dt)
        // it walks only once it faces the spot, and stops short of any heron it would come too close to
        const facing = Math.abs(wrap(want - heading)) < 0.3
        const step = facing ? S.stalk.speed * dt : 0
        const [nx, nz] = [x + Math.cos(heading) * step, z - Math.sin(heading) * step]
        if (step > 0 && pointInPolygon(nx, nz, zone.points) && clearOf(nx, nz, 0)) {
          pose.strideDistance += Math.hypot(nx - x, nz - z)
          x = nx; z = nz
        } else if (step > 0) begin('wait')
        if (Math.hypot(tx - x, tz - z) < ARRIVE) begin('wait')
        break
      }
      case 'strike': {
        strikeT += dt
        const { draw, drawNeck, down, hold } = S.strike
        pose.reachDepth = reachDepth
        pose.reachAngle = S.strike.reachAngle
        if (strikeT < draw) {
          neck = drawNeck
        } else if (strikeT < draw + hold + down) {
          // down along a smoothstep over `down`, so it builds over a few frames at any frame rate, then held
          from ??= { neck: pose.neckExtend, lean: pose.lean.x }
          const k = ease(Math.min(1, (strikeT - draw) / down))
          pose.reach = k
          pose.neckExtend = from.neck + (0.5 - from.neck) * k
          pose.lean.x = from.lean + (S.strike.lean - from.lean) * k
          if (k > 0.9 && !rang) { rang = true; ctx.onRipple?.(x + Math.cos(heading) * S.strike.ahead, z - Math.sin(heading) * S.strike.ahead, S.strike.ring) }
          pose.gait = approach(pose.gait, 0, GAIT_RATE, dt)
          pose.headPitch = approach(pose.headPitch, 0, PITCH_RATE, dt)
          return
        }
        break
      }
      case 'swallow': neck = 1; pitch = S.swallow.pitch; break
      case 'alert': neck = 1; lean = S.alert.lean; break
      case 'hunch': neck = 0; lean = S.rest.lean; break
    }
    pose.reach = approach(pose.reach, 0, 3, dt)
    pose.neckExtend = approach(pose.neckExtend, neck, NECK_RATE, dt)
    pose.headPitch = approach(pose.headPitch, pitch, PITCH_RATE, dt)
    pose.lean.x = approach(pose.lean.x, lean, LEAN_RATE, dt)
    pose.gait = approach(pose.gait, gait, GAIT_RATE, dt)
    if (state !== 'strike') { pose.reachDepth = 0; pose.reachAngle = Math.PI / 2 }
    // a gust ruffles: a tiny roll
    pose.lean.z = approach(pose.lean.z, (ctx.wind().effective - 0.3) * 0.02, 2, dt)
  }

  return controller
}

/**
 * Where a heron starts: anywhere in its zone, no closer to the herons already placed than it keeps from each (its own
 * species' spacing for one of its group, else the larger of the two birds' for another), if one of a few hundred tries
 * is. The spawner calls it with the cast's own random source, as it does startSpot for the swimmers.
 */
export function heronStart(zone: PolygonZone, rng: Rng, group: FlockGroup | undefined, spacing: HeronSettings['spacing'], placed: readonly Neighbour[]): [number, number] {
  let spot = randomPointInPolygon(zone.points, rng)
  for (let i = 0; i < 300; i++) {
    if (placed.every(n => Math.hypot(n.x - spot[0], n.z - spot[1]) >= keepFrom(n, group, spacing) + MARGIN)) return spot
    spot = randomPointInPolygon(zone.points, rng)
  }
  return spot
}
```

```ts
type Patch = Partial<Omit<PoseState, 'lean'>> & { leanX?: number }
function pose(model: DetailedBirdModel, patch: Patch): void {
  const { leanX = 0, ...rest } = patch
  const p: PoseState = { ...defaultPose(), ...rest }
  p.lean.set(leanX, 0, 0)
  model.pose(p)
}

/** The heron style's poses for a species, at their fullest, from its own settings. */
function shapes(S: HeronSettings): Record<string, Patch> {
  return {
    hunch: { neckExtend: 0, leanX: S.rest.lean },
    trace: { neckExtend: 0.5 },
    erect: { neckExtend: 1, leanX: S.alert.lean },
    swallow: { neckExtend: 1, headPitch: S.swallow.pitch },
    // the bill aimed at the water over a bed 15 cm down, as on the far shallows
    strike: { reach: 1, leanX: S.strike.lean, reachAngle: S.strike.reachAngle, reachDepth: -(0.15 - S.strike.depth), neckExtend: 0.5 },
  }
}

/** The top of the back as posed, mm: the highest skin point from the tail to the neck's base (the rig's `neckBase`). */
function backNow(model: DetailedBirdModel, neckBase: string): number {
  const rings = posedRings(model)
  const last = Math.floor(model.profile.uOf(neckBase) * (RINGS - 1))
  let top = -Infinity
  for (let i = 0; i <= last; i++) for (const v of rings[i]!) top = Math.max(top, v.y)
  return top * 1000
}

/** The crown above the top of the back, mm, in a pose. */
function crownAboveBack(spec: typeof HERON, model: DetailedBirdModel, patch: Patch): number {
  pose(model, patch)
  return crownNow(model) * 1000 - backNow(model, spec.detail!.rig.neckBase)
}

const deg = (rad: number) => (rad * 180) / Math.PI

/** Where the kink sits: its distance down the chain from the occiput, as a fraction of the neck's length along the spine. */
function kinkShare(spec: typeof HERON): number {
  const d = spec.detail!
  const profile = createProfile(d.profile)
  const [base, , kink] = d.rig.neck!
  const head = d.rig.neck!.at(-1)!
  return (profile.uOf(head) - profile.uOf(kink!)) / (profile.uOf(head) - profile.uOf(base!))
}
```
